# Publication restrictions

What may be published here, and what may never be. These rules are enforced by `scripts/check_repo.py` where a machine can enforce them and by review everywhere else. They are referenced by [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## May be published

- **Patch source written for this project:** `provider.c` native-source providers and the manifest that ships in the package, under a licence recorded in `release.json`.
- **BPS deltas** at `patches/<platform>/<game>/source/<package-id>.bps`, only when the delta contains no reconstructable game content: it is small (the check rejects more than 4 KiB), byte-identical to the `game.bps` in the released package and reviewed for what its changed bytes reveal. Whether a particular delta may be redistributed is the maintainer's decision on review; the repository does not take a position on legal clearance.
- **Digests:** SHA-256 of exact source and patched images, of packages and of sources.
- **Synthetic material:** fabricated images and packages generated by the scripts in this repository, with no relation to any commercial title.
- **Aggregate maintainer attestation** of commercial verification: an outcome such as "verified against the exact image, all listed scope" and the evidence tier, nothing more.

## May never be published

- ROMs, patched ROMs, BIOS, firmware, disc images, save files, cartridge dumps (CI rejects the common extensions and any file over 512 KiB).
- Extracted or reconstructable game data: assets, sprite sheets, tile or palette dumps, VRAM and frame dumps, audio, disassembly or decompiled source, traces and logs derived from running a commercial game.
- Screenshots or recordings of a commercial game, except aggregate project-owned documentation explicitly approved by the maintainer.
- Download links or instructions for obtaining commercial images.
- Credentials, tokens, private keys, and paths or content from the private development harness.
- Prebuilt binaries or `.segapatch` archives in git. Packages are built from source in CI and attached to a GitHub Release.

## When in doubt

Do not commit it. Open a pull request without the file and describe it in words; the maintainer decides. An accidental commit of restricted content requires a history rewrite coordinated with the maintainer, not a follow-up deletion.

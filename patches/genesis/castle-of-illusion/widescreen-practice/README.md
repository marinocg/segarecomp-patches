# Castle of Illusion Starring Mickey Mouse (Genesis): authored widescreen + Practice+

> One schema 3 / native ABI 2 mod. **Widescreen:** in the 17 verified scrolling scenes (every scrolling stage of the game) the cells beside the 320-pixel picture are rebuilt from the game's own block maps instead of repeating the wrapped VDP plane. **Actors:** the game's own sprites that its object sprite builder drops beside the picture are drawn as native ABI 2 virtual sprites from the game's own VRAM/CRAM art, on the layer of their own priority bit, so actors no longer pop in and out beside the picture (no ROM edit, no guest write). **Practice+:** pressing A+B+C together restores Mickey's power to full, only in the verified scenes, only when it is 1 to 4.

## Widescreen: how it works

The game keeps, for each of its two scrolling planes, a small record in work RAM (the scroll values, the address and size of a byte map of 16 x 16 pixel blocks, and the address of the table that expands a block to four VDP name-table words). The provider reads those records and maps through the ABI 2 memory service at `frame-end`, rebuilds the name-table words of the cells around the camera for both planes, and submits them as the typed authored `scenery_window` at `render`. The host's own VDP code decodes patterns and palettes; the provider never touches VRAM and invents no tile (a cell outside a map is the game's own empty block). The cartridge is never read at run time and nothing from it is embedded here. The picture inside the original 320 x 224 is Vanilla's.

A scene is accepted only when the records have exactly the verified shape of a running level (the addresses of both maps and both tables, and the map sizes of one of the scenes below). A menu, cutscene, level load, any other level or any other layout is declined and the host presents its generic extension for that frame. Windows are a pure function of the world cell (rows outside a map are the game's own empty block, as the VDP planes hold them). Plane A gets **two** windows (product ADR 0313), and so does plane B in the five scenes where it is authored (`a80x80`, `a32x128`, `a80x128`, whose plane B is a real world map, and `a48x160-b48`, `a96x64-b96`, see below); in the other twelve scenes plane B has no window and the VDP-derived extension answers it (product ADR 0314): the main window is 128 cells wide and 32 rows high and starts 6 cells left of the view (the view starts `left` pixels left of the camera, see "Where the picture sits in the view"; the measured need of the scrolled rows is up to 18 cells beyond the view's right edge, parallax rows included, so the window leaves 4 cells of slack on the right and 6 on the left, which absorb the camera's motion between the `frame-end` read and the frame that is produced two guest frames later), and a second window of 128 cells and 16 rows from picture row 128 down starts 2 cells left of the view in the unscrolled plane coordinates, where a split-scroll band (the status bar and, in some stages, a strip above it) is scrolled to 0 while the playfield scrolls with the camera. A cell is answered by the first window of its plane that contains it; the two ranges can be disjoint, so their union can exceed the 128-column bound of one window (up to 228 columns were measured).

## Where the picture sits in the view

At 16:9 and wider the host plans one total of extra columns for the chosen aspect (238 + 320 + 238 at 32:9). A side-scroller does not need the same amount behind the camera as ahead of it: at the first column of a level there is no world to the left. The provider therefore tells the host where to put the picture (product ADR 0315, `view_margin` / `view_left`): with `margin` the host's per-side margin, `left` is a pure function of the camera: **0 at the level's first column (nothing to the left exists), `margin` (the centred view) in the middle, `2 * margin` at its last column (camera = level width - 320, nothing to the right exists)**, with a linear ramp at each end of `zone = min(range / 2, 6 * margin)` camera pixels (at 32:9, 1,428 pixels). **Why a ramp and not `min(camera, margin)`:** the guest draws its parallax layers for its own camera, so while `left` changes the picture's layers are offset from the view's world by `(1 - k) * left` (k = the layer's speed factor). A 1:1 slide froze the world in the view while every background layer slid backwards at `(1 - k)` of the camera's speed: the dizzying parallax. With the ramp that drift is at most 1/6 of that, and `left` is constant everywhere else. A level narrower than the planned total keeps the old rule (`min(camera, margin)`). Because it is a function of the camera alone it needs no state (replay, rewind and load agree). The total, and so the display aspect, never changes; the guest, the native picture and its timing are untouched.

* **The windows follow the placement.** The provider learns `margin` in the render phase (`view_margin`), builds the windows at the next `frame-end` for the view `left` pixels left of the camera, and declares the placement at `render` only while the host's margin is still the one the windows were built for. After an aspect change the next two frames are therefore centred (never a placement computed for another aspect; the windows built for the old view answer what they cover and the generic extension the rest) and the third is placed.
* **Scenes and transitions.** A placement is declared only for a verified scene; a menu, cutscene, level load or transition is centred, as before.
* **The picture is never reinterpreted.** The host presents an asymmetric frame whole, at its own total, and validates `left` itself; if the aspect is narrowed while such a frame is on screen it shows the native picture for that frame (never a crop of the asymmetric frame), and a stale or out-of-range placement is the centred layout.
* **The Practice+ panel moved.** The fixed HUD layer sits at the left edge of the whole view, which is where the game's own power meter is when the picture is at the left of the view; the panel is now always a positioned layer anchored to the top-right corner of the whole presented view (never the fixed overlay, and so never claimed as the game's relocated HUD). At 4:3 and in Original the view is the picture, so the layer is anchored to the picture's own top-right corner; that case was not separately inspected (the host's notice covers that corner in the captured frames).
* **Right-hand boundary.** Nothing is invented at the end of a level: beyond the last map column the plane repeats its edge column exactly as before, and the placement does not depend on the map's right edge (no level-end metadata was verified, so none is used).

## Coverage (local runs on the exact image, aggregates only)

Method: the generated-native viewer program of the original image, built through `segarecomp build --native-package`, played under the dummy video driver with finite instruction budgets and a scripted pad, compared with the Vanilla program of the same image at the same budget; the viewer's own `SEGARECOMP_COVERAGE_REPORT` supplies the per-scene accounting.

| Scene | 4:3 | 16:9 | 21:9 | 32:9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a16x240-b16`, `a32x112-b32` (played, compared with Vanilla at every aspect) | identical to Vanilla; nothing requested | served; 0 pixels differ inside the picture | served; 0 inside | served; 0 inside |
| the other nine scenes (flown, modded run at 32:9 only) | not run | not run | not run | served in every frame except the first ~7 of each level start; no fault |

* **The first frames of a level are not served** (the VDP scroll registers are still being set; about five frames per level start) and the host presents the generic extension there. Frames of motion, jumps and the camera stopping at a wall had no unanswered cell in either scene (every other frame of the runs was served).
* **Where the generic extension already agrees, the output equals it**: at 16:9 only the Practice+ panel differs from Vanilla; at 21:9 and 32:9 the margins beyond what the game itself keeps in VRAM now show the true level (the generic extension repeats the wrapped plane, which is visibly wrong there, for example a platform edge repeated on the other side of Mickey).
* **Credit limitation (host rule, not a defect of the data):** the game draws its status bar with the VDP window plane on the bottom four cell rows. The host never credits "authored scenery" on a frame whose window plane owns a picture edge, so the report counts the scenery of every frame as `requested` and `missing` (0 covered) even though the rows above the status bar are served from the authored window. The status-bar rows beside the picture stay the host's generic presentation (plane A omitted there).
* **Not delivered:** per-actor identity or world position (actors are drawn from the game's own sprite pieces, see below), nothing about priority (see below: a piece takes the layer of its own priority bit), a relocated or enlarged HUD (the original status bar is left where it is), widescreen at any aspect in an unverified scene.
* **Evidence of the scenery itself** (independent of the provider): in 103 frames sampled across the first two scenes, 230,720 of 230,720 on-screen cells of both planes equal the name-table words the game itself had written into VRAM (no mismatch); cells beyond the screen that the game had already streamed agree too (84,633 equal, 543 stale because the game had not rewritten them yet).

* **The final margins are pixel-identical to the previous ones** in every compared scene (0 differing pixels over 20 sampled frames each in 17 scenes): the 653 and 532 failing frames were cells whose authored content the VDP-derived extension already shows exactly, so nothing visible changed, only the failures.
* **Is plane B world-map content, wrapped VRAM content or something else?** Over 5 scenes x 8 frames (about 90,000 off-picture cells per plane and frame), wherever the provider answered a plane B cell (playfield rows, rows scrolled to 0, status rows) its word equalled the VDP's own word in **100%** of the cells. Plane A is different on purpose: its words differ from the wrapped VRAM exactly where the world map continues beyond what the game had streamed (35 to 75% of the margin cells in the scenes examined), which is what authoring plane A is for; the far-ahead check below shows those words are right. In 12 of the 14 scenes whose plane B is a 32-block tiling map the VDP-derived margins equal the authored ones pixel for pixel, so plane B is left to the VDP there. In the other two, `a48x160-b48` (0,1) and `a96x64-b96` (4,2), they do not: the plane B map is taller than the VDP's 64-cell plane, the VDP shows wrapped (stale) rows there while the authored margins show world rows (in (0,1) only the status band rows differ: a textured strip against a plain one). Neither was proven to be what a wider screen would show, so those two scenes keep the authored plane B exactly as before, which is not a claim of fidelity.
* **A figure published earlier is withdrawn:** "the band margins equalled the generic ones in 6 of 10 sampled frames" compared the Vanilla program with the modded one at the same frame number, and the two programs are not at the same game state at the same frame number (they differ by slice timing; the same effect makes a Vanilla 4:3 comparison fail). The word-level comparison above replaces it.
* **The Practice+ panel, the virtual actors and the positioned layers do not depend on the scenery** (product ADR 0313): a scenery cell fault only fails the frame's authored-scenery claim. Before, every failing frame also lost the panel and the actors (1,893 frames; in the water stage the dithered enemies and pickups beside the picture vanished during the fault run).
* **Causes of the 110 frames left** (the per-frame need of the host against the windows): the first few frames of a level, in every scene (the provider's vertical scroll, read at `frame-end`, leads the VDP's, about 5 frames per level start), and in `a16x64` a short transition. There is no failure with a persistent cause left in the 17 scenes of this sweep.
* **Why plane B's record could not be used (stage (2,0)):** the playfield rows of plane B are scrolled by the per-line table, not by the camera in plane B's record (a record camera of 0 against a VDP scroll of -482, 60 cells away); the game mirrors that table in work RAM at an address that was not stable across stages and frames, so reading it was not an option. Leaving the plane to the VDP made the question moot, because the VDP-derived extension is already what the authored one reproduced.

## Actors beside the picture (virtual sprites with the game's own art)

The game's object sprite builder assembles each sprite piece's hardware entry (y, size, link, attribute) in its work-RAM sprite buffer, then asks one routine whether the piece's x is inside the range it keeps (sprite x `0x60..0x1DF`, screen -32..351) and silently drops it otherwise, unless its object is flagged never-clipped. That is why actors vanish and appear 32 pixels beside the picture however wide the presentation is.

A `before` function hook on that routine observes every piece at that moment (registers `d0` = x, `a0` = the object, `a2` = the buffer position after the attribute), before the decision. A piece the game would drop is read back from the buffer (y, size, attribute) and submitted at `render` as a `GENESIS_VSPRITE_GAME_STATE_VERIFIED` virtual sprite in presentation space: the tile, palette and flips come from the attribute word and the host decodes the art from the frame's own VRAM/CRAM (the package copies no art and reads no ROM). Pieces the game drops are submitted whole. A piece the game keeps but that reaches beside the 320 px raster (the 32 columns each side that the sprite table can address and the hardware never shows) is submitted too, clipped to the part outside the raster and flagged `GENESIS_VSPRITE_FLAG_HARDWARE_PIECE` (product ADR 0313): it is in the game's own sprite table, so a host whose extension carries the hardware sprite layer (Level 3) already draws it with the original evaluation rules and omits the command (a duplicate would be ordered against priority classes, not against individual hardware sprites, and would reveal what the hardware truncated), while a host without that layer (Level 2) draws it as submitted, which completes actors that straddle the picture edge (without it an actor there is drawn as a fragment). Measured on real scenes (aggregates only): at Level 3 the output of the final provider is pixel-identical to the provider that submits dropped pieces only (0 differing pixels in four scenes, 20 frames each), and at Level 2 it equals the provider that submitted the kept pieces unflagged. The picture inside 320 x 224 is untouched.

**Budget (intentional distinction).** The set uses the extended sprite budget: the original per-scanline limits (20 sprites, 40 cells in a 40-cell picture) belong to the *guest's hardware rendering*, which this package never changes (the native picture is identical, see below); the actors beside the picture are *presentation* of pieces the game itself dropped, so they are not subject to a budget the hardware never applied to them. Demand stays bounded and observable: at most 48 pieces per frame by construction (the largest measured frame had 23 commands, in 9 real stages at both levels), the product counts the rows whose total demand exceeds the original limits (`rows_over_sprite_limit`, `rows_over_cell_budget` in the extension result), and in the examined stages that happened only in the jack-in-the-box boss arena (384 rows over the sprite limit and 672 over the cell budget across the run at Level 2, at most 16 rows in one frame; 60 rows over the cell budget at Level 3). Under the original `limit` budget those rows would drop or truncate an actor beside the picture. The submission is bound by the host to the frame epoch (the product's `frame.work_ram` gate), so a stale set, a restore or a rewind never draws game art. Nothing is written to the guest and no guest code is changed: a bounded Vanilla-versus-Modded run with the hook linked is **identical to Vanilla** in frames, work RAM and the audio stream (the earlier ROM edit changed the audio stream; it was removed).

**Priority.** Each piece is submitted on the layer that matches its own hardware priority bit (bit 15 of the attribute word the builder wrote): priority 0 -> `BETWEEN` (above low-priority planes and sprites, below high-priority ones), priority 1 -> `ABOVE_ALL`. Beside the picture the host orders a `BETWEEN` piece against the priority class its extension producer recorded for that very pixel (product ADR 0312), so a low-priority actor stays behind a high-priority plane and shows through its transparent openings exactly as in the picture, with no seam at the picture edge. Aggregates from private real-image runs (no image data): in the red-water stage the 331 sets that were rejected when `BETWEEN` was first tried compose, 0 are rejected in any examined stage, and in 3,732 virtual-sprite pixels checked against an independent decode of the frame's own VDP state, 1,994 are hidden behind high-priority water and 1,738 drawn through its openings, with no disagreement; in a stage with foreground blocks an actor behind a block is hidden by it (the previous `ABOVE_ALL` submission drew it over the block, which the game never does). A host without class data (a product older than ADR 0312) rejects such a set whole (fail closed) and shows no actor beside the picture; the 4:3 picture is unchanged.

* **Verified actors (local real-image runs, aggregates only):**
  * The heart pickup is a real animated actor: tracked frame by frame at 32:9 it moves continuously across the game's own clip boundary (screen -32) out to -208 px (80 px beyond the hardware's -128 limit), and in all 27 sampled frames where hearts were both inside the picture and beyond the clip, every heart showed the same animation art in the same frame.
  * Four to five distinct object animation tables (several actor kinds, not one) contribute dropped pieces in each of the stages examined. The package depends only on the builder interface (the clip routine's registers and the sprite-buffer entry), never on an object's internal layout.
  * Oracle: with the previous widened-clip ROM edit as an independent reference (hardware sprites reach screen -127..383), the virtual-sprite output equalled the reference pixel for pixel in the bands -127..-33 and 352..383 in all 100 sampled frames across the stages (0,0), (0,2), (1,1), (1,0) and (3,0) at 32:9 (487,680 band pixels per stage, 31 pixels differing in a single frame of one stage).
* **Aspects:** 4:3 is unchanged (nothing is requested); 16:9 and 21:9 show actors up to their margins (heart tracks to -100 px at 21:9); 32:9 shows them up to its 238-pixel margin. The right side of the hardware range (screen > 383) was covered by the compositor and synthetic tests and by pieces at 352..383 in the oracle; **no real actor was observed beyond 383 in the examined runs.**
* **Limits (stated, not hidden):** at most 48 pieces per frame, coordinates within +/-900 px, only in the verified scenes, only pieces the game builds this frame (an actor the game does not process is not drawn), **objects that do not exist yet are not drawn: the game creates its objects only near the camera (in the swimming arena, (3,5) to (3,7), a private frame-by-frame object-table log showed new objects appearing a fixed ~190 px from the camera, and they first show at the right edge of the native picture), so on a wide view an object can appear inside the extended area instead of entering from its edge. Whether and how the guest could create objects earlier without changing its behavior has **not been established** (the investigation is incomplete; one candidate, the game's block-based "is the object on screen" window, was tried as an experiment and did not move the spawn point), and this package does nothing about it, priority is that of the piece's own bit and is only as exact as the product's recorded class of the margin pixel (see "Priority"), the sprite budget is the extended one (see "Budget").
* **Presentation only.** Under Comfort the host keeps virtual pixels only on the rows it serves from the provider; margin pixels are never credited as presented under Comfort or Borders (the counters under-claim).

## Bosses and cutscenes

Examined with the same debug build, at 32:9:

* **Boss arenas** (forest tree boss at (0,5), the jack-in-the-box boss at (1,3), the tower and other boss arenas that load through the table above): the arenas whose map shape is in the verified table (`a16x32-b16`, `a17x32-b17`, ...) render with authored margins (the generic extension repeats slabs and misplaces platform edges there); boss and Mickey sprites are the game's own, in the picture. Boss arenas with another shape ((2,3), (3,5), ...) are declined and shown by the generic extension unchanged. Nothing was rendered incorrectly *by this package* in the arenas that were examined; the declined ones were not judged for generic-extension quality.
* **Cutscenes, logos, title, menus and level transitions** (both scroll records zeroed): the package does nothing there. In 11 sampled frames across the SEGA logo, intro cutscene, title and copyright screens the modded frame was **pixel-identical to Vanilla** at 32:9. Those screens are the host's generic extension, which is visibly imperfect for them (slivers of the wrapped planes beside the framed picture, gaps beside the cutscene viewport). I tried offering empty windows there to make the margins plain backdrop; because the cutscenes' scroll values are not among the records this package reads, the result was worse in several frames than the generic extension, so it was removed. A proper fix needs those scenes' scroll sources (or a change to the generic extension) and is **not done**.
* Pale square tiles at the top-left margin of `(0,5)` are plane B map content (confirmed by blanking plane B), not a rendering error of the scenery path; the same colour appears in the native picture.

## Practice+

| | |
|---|---|
| Gesture | **A + B + C together** on controller 1 (Start is never used). One restoration per press: holding the chord does nothing more, and a chord that was already down when a level started never fires later. **While a verified level runs the complete chord is taken away from what the game reads** (`input.intercept`), so Practice+ never makes Mickey jump or attack; buttons that arrive a frame before the chord is complete still reach the game, and nothing is intercepted outside the two scenes |
| Where | only in the verified scenes (the same records and map shapes as the widescreen check, read again at the moment of the press). The chord is Practice+'s there even at full power, where it does nothing |
| When | only while Mickey's power is 1 to 4. At 0 (dead) or at 5 (full) nothing is written |
| Effect | power (work RAM `0xF322`) becomes 5, the five HUD slot words (`0xF244`..`0xF24D`) and the HUD upload flag (bit 0 of `0xF409`) are set exactly as the game's own heal routine leaves them, so the on-screen power row updates. Seven staged writes, three declared read-write ranges. Lives, items, score and everything else are untouched |
| Why these facts | power runs 0..5 (the game's own routines cap it at 5 and its HUD has five slots; observed: a value above 5 written into RAM is brought back to 5 by the game); a direct write to the power word alone leaves the HUD stale until the next game event, which is why the HUD words and flag are written too. No function hook was needed |
| Indicator | a small "P+" panel (with the number of restorations and a "POWER" notice for 1.5 seconds after one) in a positioned layer anchored to the top-right corner of the whole presented view (the picture can sit at the left edge of the view, where the game's own power meter is, so the panel never goes there). Seen with the chord: "P+1" and a "POWER" notice there, then "P+1". At 4:3 and in Original the layer is the picture's own top-right corner (not separately inspected) |
| State | the previous chord state, the counter and the notice timer are in the mod state: save states, rewind and replay reproduce them |

**Practice+ changes the game** (it is a practice aid, not a presentation effect) and it is the only part of this package that does. Widescreen alone never writes guest memory.

## What was verified (local, real image) and what was not

**Review-fix validation of the asymmetric view (final package `13300ec3…`, local runs on the exact image, aggregates only).**

* *Practice+ chord with the relocated panel:* power 3 set at frame 5900, the chord at 5950: power 5, the five HUD slots restored, exactly the 7 declared writes (`committed_writes` 7), no fault; the panel shows "P+1" and the "POWER" notice in the top-right corner of the 32:9 view, then "P+1".

* *Movement in both directions and a level transition:* four stages at 32:9 (right, down, right, left, release; camera 0 to 479): 30 of 31 verified frames have the picture at exactly `min(camera, margin)`; the 31st is a level-transition frame (the previous level's records, an unverified scene), correctly centred.
* *Guest state:* work RAM, VRAM and CRAM are byte-identical at two frame boundaries (6400 and 7390) between this package on the final product runtime and the previous (symmetric) package on the previous product runtime, same input script: the placement changes nothing but the presentation. * *Not verified:* the right-hand level boundary (not reached naturally; no verified level-state setup exists, so no right-boundary behaviour is claimed); Original's panel corner; launcher flow beyond the deterministic package (two packs identical; the repository checks pass).

**Early object activation (investigation, no variant shipped).** The remaining limitation is that layout objects are created only up to about 512 px ahead of the camera (a streamer that loads one 128 px section column at camera + 384 whenever the camera's section changes), so a wide view shows no layout objects in its rightmost ~285 px until the next section step; the objects that appear inside the view at a constant ~190 px are created by object AI, not by the layout. The streamer's window is not one number: the column loader's lookahead, the initial level fill (a loop bound), the row loader's column span and the per-object off-screen deletion test (which deletes an object when it is more than 512 px ahead, and a deleted object only returns when its section column is loaded again) all encode the same window, and objects live in fixed pools (32, 32 and 10). Private builds on the exact image with the lookahead alone, with the lookahead plus the deletion limit, and with all four constants raised to a 896 px window were compared with the original run in eight stages with exact allocation counters: none reached the goal safely (objects the original run creates were never created in several stages, 3 of 4 in stage 1,0 and 38 of 60 in 3,7; objects still appeared inside the 32:9 view; with all four constants the 10-slot pool was full 14 times in stage 4,0, a failed allocation the original game never has). A static ROM edit therefore **cannot be shipped as a safe variant from a few immediates**; the product supports the packaging (a native package that also carries a typed ROM edit builds, installs, approves and runs over the verified edited image, proven end to end on a fabricated image by marinocg/segarecomp#241), but the activation change itself would need a coordinated redesign of the streamer's window and the deletion rule that this evidence does not justify. This package therefore stays presentation-only, as designed.

**Follow-up: coordinated activation variants (separate projects, gameplay-changing).** A later investigation attributed the losses of the static edits to individual constants (a section-list limit and the off-screen deletion limit, plus pool-full allocation failures at the widest windows) and found that a coordinated window with a bounded guest-asm retry of failed section loads is safe over 19 stages at 21:9: see [`castle-of-illusion-activation-21x9`](../activation-21x9/README.md) and [`castle-of-illusion-activation-16x9`](../activation-16x9/README.md). They are separate packages with their own identities and never part of this presentation-only package, which is unchanged.

**The asymmetric view (product ADR 0315; local runs on the exact image, aggregates only, level-select and aspect-switch seams in a private runtime copy, nothing committed).** Each run plays a stage with the product's real presenter (software renderer) at 16:9, 21:9 or 32:9, with Right held, then Down, Right, Left and a release, and captures the presented window plus a dump of the VDP and work-RAM state at the same frames.
* *The picture sits where the rule says:* in 78 frames of verified scenes (six stages at 32:9, two at 21:9 and 16:9; camera 0 to 960; moving right, stopped, moving left, back at the level's start) the native picture was found, by matching 320 columns against an independent decode of the dumped VDP state (no product code), at exactly `min(camera, margin)` every time: 0 at a level's first column, the intermediate values (13, 56, 116, 120, 134, 211, 215) while the camera is inside the margin, and the margin itself (238 at 32:9, 101 at 21:9, 39 at 16:9, the centred view) beyond it. 0 frames disagreed. The two frames of a level transition that were sampled (plane records of the previous level, a fade) have no verifiable picture; the one that was inspected is centred, as an unverified scene must be.
* *Nothing but the placement changed:* against the previous provider on the same product build (the symmetric view; 56 frames in three stages and three aspects) the columns both views show are identical, 0 differing pixels of 2,299,341 compared (every third row; the top 16 rows' outer 60 columns are excluded: the Practice+ panel moved to the top right and the host's notice sits there). Scenery is credited exactly as before (the whole-frame counters are the same numbers in both builds).
* *The world ahead is the game's own:* the columns that exist only in the asymmetric view (238 at a level's first column, 122 at camera 116) were compared with plane A decoded straight from the game's own map and block table in the dumped work RAM, plane B and the hardware sprites from the dumped VDP state: 41,976 pixels in stages (0,0) and (2,0), 0 differing. * *An aspect change while playing* (the real settings path, camera held at 0, 32:9 -> 16:9 -> 32:9 -> 21:9, every frame around each switch captured): when the new aspect is narrower (32:9 -> 16:9, 32:9 -> 21:9) the one frame produced under the old plan meets the new plan as the **native picture only** (never a crop of the asymmetric surface); when it is wider (16:9 -> 32:9) that frame is shown whole at its own width.

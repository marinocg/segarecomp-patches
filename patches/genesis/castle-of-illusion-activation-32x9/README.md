# Castle of Illusion: object activation with deferral for 32:9 (GAMEPLAY-CHANGING)

An opt-in variant, separate from the presentation-only package [`castle-of-illusion`](../castle-of-illusion/README.md), which stays available as the unmodified-gameplay alternative and is never modified by this one. It shares that package's provider byte for byte (checked by `tests/test_castle_activation.py`) and, with the 16:9 and 21:9 variants, ONE generated guest routine (`scripts/gen_castle_retry.py`; the aspects differ only in the section lookaheads: right 0x2c0 / left 0x180 here). It **changes which layout objects exist and when** (more enemies and items alive earlier and farther away): explicitly **gameplay-changing**. The original object pools, initialization routines and game logic are used as they are; no pool is enlarged, no object is evicted or suppressed, nothing is replaced.

## The activation policy
* **Nine typed ROM edits** (`edits/activation.edits`, every site asserts its original value): right lookahead 0x180 -> 0x2c0, left 0x71 -> 0x180, initial-fill bound 0x280 -> 0x4c0, row-loader span 4 -> 6, five six-byte hooks (the fail hook lives at 0x60d40).
* **Deferral (entry hook of the section loader).** A section is deferred when ALL hold: the layout pool (0xD400..0xDBFF, 32 slots) holds >= 20 objects; its column lies beyond the ORIGINAL window (right of `(camera+0x180)>>7`, left of `(camera-0xF1)>>7`); and fewer than **12** of the 16 ring slots are in use. Deferred = queued, and the hook returns exactly as the loader would after loading nothing. The decision reads only the guest camera word and the pool, never host presentation. A deferred section keeps its age while it waits, is revisited once per streamer invocation, loads when the pool has room, and is dropped when it leaves the retry window (the original would not have loaded it either, and the game's own streamer asks again on re-entry). In-window sections are never deferred.
* **Queue reservation.** The ring is 16 slots; speculative deferrals may take at most 12, **the last 4 are for genuine allocation failures** (the fail hook may use all 16). Past the cap a speculative section is simply loaded as the original would. This is the smallest correction found: no second queue and no scheduler. Tested: with 12 speculative slots in use an original-window section that fails to allocate is still queued; more speculative demand then loads instead of queueing; nothing is dropped.
* **No eviction.** Freeing a live object through the original free path (0x1c94) was not implemented: no object class with an established lifecycle/reactivation semantics was identified, and the policy above proved sufficient. A section is only ever deferred before it consumes a slot.
* What the policy cannot know is whether a deferred section holds a layout object, a non-visual trigger or a spawner; it treats sections alike and relies on revisiting, not discarding.

## Injected-code equivalence (CCR, registers, RAM)
* **The original loader has exactly two callers** (0x6d5ac, 0x6d604). Both continue with `ADDI.W #$80,Dn`, which rewrites X,N,Z,V,C, and neither reads d3, d6 or a3 afterwards (verified against the image; d4 is live and never touched). So at the loader entry and at the early return of a deferral the whole CCR and d3/d6/a3 are dead.
* **Still, the decision is X-neutral**: it uses only LEA/MOVEA/MOVE/ROR/ANDI/CMP/CMPI/TST/DBRA (no ADD/SUB/shift-with-X); counters in the entry and fail paths are LEA+MOVE as well. At the fail-hook continuation the CCR is dead too (LEA/DBRA, then every use is preceded by a flag-setting instruction) but the hook reproduces it exactly anyway. The frame hook runs extra original-loader calls and cannot preserve X (the product assembler has no ADDX/ROXL and forbids SR access); X is dead there on every executed path measured (forcing X to 0 versus 1 gave a bit-identical machine digest over 3,002 frames), **not proven**; a pinned test documents it.
* **Executable fixture** (`tests/test_castle_activation_exec.py`, 39 scenarios per variant, 117 in total): exact stop path, every register, SP, **the CCR where it is live** (fail hook: exact for every entry CCR and every exit; reset/cull/frame: X preserved), the **caller-visible CCR after its own ADDI is identical whether the section was deferred or loaded and independent of the entry flags**, and the complete RAM image outside the documented extent. A fixture stub bug found on the way (the stub loader's success branch skipped 6 instead of 8 bytes and executed a jump operand as `MOVE.W D0,D1`) is fixed. Mutations caught: threshold, reservation cap, retry ageing, stamp.
* **RAM ownership.** The retry state is 0xFFFC00..0xFFFC8F (key, flags, 16 x 8-byte slots), the diagnostics 0xFFFCA0..0xFFFCB3 (words): 0xB4 bytes in total, no write anywhere else (asserted statically by address and dynamically by the full-RAM hash). A linear decode of the image's code region found no absolute-address operand in 0xFFFC00..0xFFFCFF (the only matches are fixed-point immediates such as `#$fffc0000`); this is a heuristic, not a proof over indirect addressing. The reset stack pointer is 0xFFFF00 and the state begins 0x300 bytes below it (0xFFFC00): it would be overwritten only if the game's stack grew deeper than 0x300 bytes; the maximum stack depth was not measured.

## Restore investigation (closed)
The earlier "1 px camera discrepancy after a quick-save restore" was **not a guest-state issue**: the canonical machine digest (CPU, RAM, VDP, devices, Z80, sound) of a restored replay equals the uninterrupted run at every frame boundary for the original, the edits-only, a pass-through injection, and every activation variant (boundary and mid-frame barriers). Repeating it on **stage 4-0 with constant input** in the real viewer (21:9 and 32:9 packages): from the second real frame after a quick-load every presented frame is bit-identical to the uninterrupted run (native picture and margins); only the synthetic presentation frame and the first real frame differ, in the **margins only**. No 1 px shift of the native picture appears at any frame. The earlier observation is explained by **capture/input alignment**: the viewer's pad script is keyed by host poll, not by guest frame, so a restored replay receives different input than the uninterrupted reference unless input is constant. The one-frame margin fallback is **intentional fail-closed behaviour**: the host accepts the provider's work-RAM snapshot only if its stamp is exactly one frame older than the frame being produced (`compat_ram_stamp + 1 == virtual_frame_count`); a restore rewinds the virtual frame counter, so the first frame after a load fails the check and the host reports `unverified_state` and presents its generic margins for that single frame. No runtime fix and no product PR: no defect was demonstrated.

## Sprite presentation refusals (actual host reason codes)
All sampled `virtual_sprite_unpresented` (reason 6) refusals, across the 16:9, 21:9 and 32:9 packages on stages 4-0, 3-2 and 1-1, came through the composed-set branch with `pic_px = 0`, side pixels only (`left_px` or `right_px` > 0), the surface visible (`ext_visible = 1`) and the side treatment **COMFORT** (`treat = 1`): by ADR 0307 a Comfort fill replaces generic-scenery columns and discards sprites drawn there. They are 0.15% of frames, **identical per stage for every package and aspect** (13 in 4-0), and occur in the level-start/transition frames. They are not caused by sprite placement.

## Acceptance (what was and was not done)
* **Smoke (real viewer, private image, dummy video/audio):** **87 of 87** runs (29 stages x the 16:9, 21:9 and 32:9 final packages) reached 6,000 frames with no guest stop and no mod fault (outcome `window_closed`). This is a smoke test, not gameplay acceptance. Replay: two independent runs of each final activation package on stages 4-0 and 3-2 gave an identical final RAM SHA-256; quick save, quick load and rewind reported `ok` with no mod fault; install, approval and variant selection through the product CLI succeeded for all four final identities and the viewers built from them ran.
* **Provider (shared):** 45 provider tests; measured over all 29 stages at 32:9 with an aspect-switch script (21:9, 16:9, Original, 32:9 mid-run): maximum **36** submitted commands per frame (host limit 64), maximum 61 candidates (scratch 128), the exact selection removed up to 31 candidates in one frame, **0 visible-command losses**. The 8 px visibility tolerance is applied only to edges that are not the picture's own; exact (zero-tolerance) edges are tested separately.
* **Not done:** item collection, damage by cause, collisions, non-visual triggers, RNG effects and unintended repeated rewards (the scripted routes collect nothing); boss fights and natural level progression (stage pair 1-3 is the clown arena, 4-3 an armored-boss arena, 5-0 the throne room by their pictures, but no fight was completed and the red-water level could not be told apart by pictures); rendered actor continuity at pixel level for the activation variants; native-picture identity between a packaged and an unpackaged program (the viewer's poll-keyed input makes two different binaries non-comparable). The 32:9 package is not a claim of full gameplay correctness.
